My Story
Built from the ground up

I ran cross country at 15 and hit a 22 minute 5K in 5 months — but I was injury prone the whole time. Knee, back, hip, shin. I pushed through it because I didn't know any better. Then I stopped running and lost everything I built.

At 22, I'm rebuilding it the right way. I found Dr. Phil Maffetone's MAF method and it changed how I think about training entirely. Every run at or below 153 bpm. Walk when I need to. Build the aerobic base before touching any speedwork.

I also run the Run! Not Gasp Run Club in the East Bay — a free Saturday morning group built on Zone 2 training and the belief that running slow is the fastest way to get fast.

This site is my training journal. The numbers, the setbacks, the progress. All of it. Smile through pain.

Why I Train This Way
The MAF Method
01
The Aerobic Base
Your aerobic system uses oxygen and fat to produce energy. When it's underdeveloped your body compensates by going anaerobic — burning sugar, producing lactic acid, getting winded fast. Building this base is the most important thing a runner can do. Everything else — speed, endurance, recovery — depends on it.
02
The Grey Zone Problem
Most runners live in the grey zone — too hard to build aerobic fitness, too easy to build real speed. It feels productive but it isn't. You accumulate fatigue without getting adaptation. Maffetone calls this aerobic deficiency syndrome. I had it. Most people do.
03
The 80/20 Rule
80% of every week's running is at or below MAF heart rate. 20% can be harder — but only after the base is built. Every elite endurance athlete in the world trains this way, from Eliud Kipchoge to Jakob Ingebrigtsen. Run slow to race fast.
04
Why It's Personal
I have gastritis, a history of injuries in four areas, and spent years running too hard and getting hurt. The MAF method is the only training approach that builds fitness while letting my body heal. Low intensity means low cortisol. Low cortisol means less inflammation. Less inflammation means my gut calms down and my connective tissue adapts.

Staying on the Road
Injury Prevention Workouts
🦵 Knee Protocol
Ben Patrick · ATG
Tibialis raises
3 × 20 reps · Daily
Back against wall, heels forward. Raise toes as high as possible, hold 2 sec, lower slowly. Foundation of knee health.
ATG split squat
3 × 10 each leg · 3x/week
Lunge position, drive front knee as far over toes as possible. Start shallow, build depth over weeks.
Clamshells with band
3 × 15 each side · 3x/week
Lie on side, band above knees. Rotate top knee up keeping feet together. Targets glute med — controls knee tracking.
Step-downs
3 × 12 each leg · 3x/week
Stand on step, lower other foot toward floor with 3–4 second count. Knee must track over second toe — no inward collapse.
🔵 Back Protocol
McGill Big Three · Daily
McGill curl-up
3 × 8 reps · Hold 10 sec · Daily
Lie on back, hands under lower back to maintain curve. Lift only head and shoulders slightly. Never flatten the spine.
Bird dog
3 × 8 each side · Hold 10 sec · Daily
On hands and knees, extend opposite arm and leg parallel to floor. Hold 10 sec. Don't let hips rotate.
Side plank
3 × 30 seconds each side · Daily
Propped on forearm, feet stacked, hips lifted. Targets quadratus lumborum — the deep lateral back muscle most runners never train.
Kneeling hip flexor stretch
60 sec each side · Daily evening
Kneeling lunge, tuck pelvis, squeeze glute of back leg. Tight hip flexors are the #1 driver of lower back pain in runners.
🟡 Hip Protocol
Dicharry · Starrett
Single leg glute bridge
3 × 15 each leg · Hold 2 sec · 3x/week
Lie on back, one knee bent, other leg straight. Push through heel, lift hips to straight line. Squeeze glute hard at top.
Lateral band walks
3 × 20 steps each direction · 3x/week
Band above knees, slight squat. Walk sideways maintaining constant tension. Targets glute medius — controls lateral hip stability during every running stride.
90/90 hip stretch
60 sec each position · Daily evening
Both legs at 90 degrees, one forward one to side. Lean forward over front leg. Addresses both hip rotation directions.
🟢 Shin & Ankle
Ben Patrick · Dicharry
Tibialis raises
3 × 20 · Daily everywhere
Can be done anywhere — waiting for kettle, bus stop, TV. The more frequent the better. Directly prevents shin splints.
Eccentric calf raises
3 × 15 · 4 seconds down · 3x/week
Rise on two feet, lower on one foot slowly counting 4 seconds. Builds Achilles and calf tendon resilience for running impact.
Single leg balance progression
3 × 30 sec each leg · Daily
Week 1: eyes open. Week 2: eyes closed. Week 3: on folded towel. Week 4: towel + eyes closed. Builds ankle proprioception.
Cadence drill
5 min per run · 170–175 bpm
Metronome app set to 170. Run to the beat — stride shortens, foot lands under hips. Reduces shin impact by up to 20%.
