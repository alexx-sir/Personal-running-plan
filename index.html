What is MAF?
Maximum Aerobic Function — Dr. Phil Maffetone's method using HR to build a deep aerobic base before adding speed.
🔥
Fat as Fuel
Below MAF ceiling you burn primarily fat. Above it you shift to glycogen — generating more stress and fatigue.
⏳
The Grey Zone
Most runners spend too much time at moderate intensity — too hard to recover, too easy to stimulate. MAF fixes this.
📈
The Proof
Mark Allen trained so slowly cyclists stopped to ask if he was okay. A year later he was faster at MAF HR than ever before.

Why slow running feels harder than running fast

When you run below your natural comfortable rhythm, you're fighting your own biomechanics. Your body has a preferred stride frequency — running significantly slower feels clunky and uncoordinated.

Think of it like driving at 15 mph in a gear designed for 40 mph. At very slow paces you also spend more time on one foot per stride, demanding more stability from muscles not yet trained for it.

The awkwardness is temporary. As your base develops your natural pace drops into MAF range — instead of shuffling at 17:00/mile, you'll cruise at 12:00/mile at the same heart rate.

The body adapts at the cellular level — building mitochondrial density, improving fat oxidation, developing capillary networks. None of this feels like progress. It is.

Why your pace varies at the same heart rate

HR reflects the total demand on your body — not just running. Sleep, heat, stress, hydration, and cumulative fatigue all change how hard your heart works at a given pace.

A poor night's sleep raises resting HR by 5–10 bpm. Heat forces your heart to cool your skin on top of fueling your muscles. HR is the truth. Pace is just the output.

When your MAF pace is slower than usual, your body is telling you something. Listen to it — the same way you should have listened to that knee last week.
