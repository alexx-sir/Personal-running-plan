What is MAF?
Maximum Aerobic Function — a method by Dr. Phil Maffetone that uses HR to build a deep aerobic base before adding speed work.
🔥
Fat as Fuel
Below your MAF ceiling, your body burns primarily fat. Above it, you shift to carbohydrates — burning through glycogen and generating more stress.
⏳
The Grey Zone
Most recreational runners spend too much time at moderate intensity — too hard to recover, too easy to stimulate. MAF fixes this.
📈
The Proof
Mark Allen trained so slowly cyclists asked if he was okay. A year later he was running faster at MAF HR than ever before at all-out effort.

Why slow running feels harder than running fast

This is the question every MAF runner asks in the first few weeks. When you run below your natural comfortable rhythm, you're fighting your own biomechanics. Your body has a preferred stride frequency designed for efficiency — running significantly slower than that feels clunky and uncoordinated.

Think of it like driving at 15 mph in a gear designed for 40 mph. The engine is working harder, not less. At very slow paces you also spend more time on one foot per stride, which demands more stability from muscles that haven't been trained for it yet.

The awkwardness is temporary. As your aerobic base develops, your natural comfortable pace drops into MAF range — instead of shuffling at 17:00/mile, you'll cruise at 12:00/mile at the same heart rate.

The body is also adapting at the cellular level — building mitochondrial density, improving fat oxidation, developing new capillary networks. None of this feels like progress. It is.

Why your pace varies at the same heart rate

Heart rate reflects the total demand on your body — not just running. Sleep, heat, stress, hydration, cumulative fatigue, and even time of day all change how hard your heart has to work to maintain a given pace.

A poor night's sleep raises resting HR by 5–10 bpm and makes every pace feel harder. Heat forces your heart to pump extra blood to your skin to cool you down on top of fueling your muscles. This is why MAF uses HR as the guide instead of pace — HR is the truth, pace is just the output.

When your MAF pace is slower than usual at the same HR, your body is telling you something. The slower pace on those days isn't weakness — it's your system correctly protecting itself.
